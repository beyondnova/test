# DMCloser — Phase 2: Brand Strategy & Conversion Copy

## 1. Brand Positioning Statement

For small Instagram and WhatsApp sellers in Morocco and MENA who lose sales because they reply slowly or off-tone, **DMCloser** is a reply assistant that generates three ready-to-send replies in the exact language your customer wrote in — including Darija — so you close more chats without typing the same answers over and over. Unlike generic AI writers built for English knowledge workers, DMCloser is built for DM sellers and actually speaks how your customers do.

---

## 2. Ideal Customer Profile

**Who**
- Solo founder or 1–3 person boutique team
- Runs the store from her phone
- Answers every customer DM personally, including nights and weekends

**Where**
- Primary: Morocco (Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier)
- Secondary: Algeria, Tunisia, Egypt, GCC, French-speaking Africa, MENA diaspora in France/Belgium/Canada

**What she sells**
- Fashion, modest wear, abayas, jewelry
- Beauty, skincare, perfume, cosmetics
- Home goods, zellige, artisanal crafts
- Average order value 200–1,500 MAD

**How she operates**
- Instagram Shop + WhatsApp Business, no Shopify or full website
- 20–200 DMs per day in mixed Darija / French / Arabic / English
- Cash on delivery, bank transfer, sometimes CMI
- One delivery partner, manual confirmation calls

**Pain points**
- Retyping the same answers about price, stock, delivery, and returns
- Missing late-night leads that go cold by morning
- Sounding rushed or inconsistent when 40 chats pile up
- Struggling to switch tone between hesitant buyers and ready-to-pay ones
- Not confident her written French or MSA reads well

**Explicitly not the ICP**
- Large brands with a customer-service team
- Shopify-native D2C stores with no DM channel
- Agencies and B2B SaaS companies
- Anyone who wants full automation / autopilot replies

## 5. Value Proposition Bullets

- **Replies in seconds, not minutes.** Paste the customer message, get 3 options. Copy the one you like.
- **Your customer's language.** Darija in Arabic script, French, standard Arabic, or English — pick per reply.
- **On-brand every time.** Fill your store profile once (delivery zones, payment, returns, FAQ) and every reply stays consistent.
- **Four tones, one click.** Friendly, professional, luxury, or direct — switch to match the chat.
- **Goal-aware.** Answer a question, handle an objection, follow up, close the sale, or upsell. You choose, we phrase.
- **You stay in control.** Every reply is a draft you copy and send — nothing is sent automatically.
- **No setup theatre.** No WhatsApp API, no extension, no chatbot flows. Works on your phone's browser while you're in Instagram.
- **Price fit for solo sellers.** Free tier for light users. Flat monthly Pro plan when you grow.

## 6. Tone of Voice Guide

**What DMCloser sounds like**
- **Practical.** We talk about chats, stock, delivery, and getting paid — not "AI-powered transformation."
- **Direct.** Short sentences. Verbs early. No filler words like "leverage," "empower," "seamlessly."
- **Respectful of the seller's time.** Every paragraph must earn its space. If a sentence doesn't sell or clarify, cut it.
- **Locally aware.** We know what COD, Darija, livraison, zellige, and "shi haja" mean. We write like someone who sells in Morocco, not someone who read about Morocco.
- **Warm, not cutesy.** Human, but not emoji-heavy or fake-friendly.
- **Confident, not boastful.** State the benefit; skip the superlatives.

**What DMCloser never sounds like**
- Silicon Valley demo-day pitch ("revolutionizing customer engagement")
- Enterprise AI vendor ("leveraging large language models to drive outcomes")
- Hype influencer ("10x your DMs!!!")
- Hand-wavy inclusive ("AI for everyone, everywhere")
- Chatbot cliché ("Hi, I'm your 24/7 AI assistant 🤖")

**Word choices**
| Use                   | Avoid                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| "reply"               | "response generation"                  |
| "customer"            | "user" / "lead"                        |
| "close the sale"      | "drive conversions"                    |
| "DM"                  | "direct messaging channel"             |
| "Darija"              | "Moroccan dialect"                     |
| "your store"          | "your business entity"                 |
| "in seconds"          | "in record time" / "lightning-fast"    |
| "free plan"           | "freemium tier"                        |

**Sentence shape**
- Headlines: 5–9 words, one idea.
- Subheads: one sentence, two at most.
- Body: one thought per paragraph. Bullets over prose when listing.

## 7. Homepage Messaging Hierarchy

Order matters — each section answers the question the previous one provoked.

1. **Hero (above the fold)**
   - Headline
   - Subheadline
   - Primary CTA: *Start free — 15 replies/month*
   - Secondary CTA: *See it in Darija* (scroll anchor or demo)
   - Trust line under CTAs: *No credit card. No WhatsApp API. Works from your phone browser.*

2. **Problem framing** — one sentence the reader nods at.
   *"Between answering the same five questions and typing replies in Darija, French, and English, you lose hours — and sometimes lose the sale."*

3. **How it works — three steps, screenshots or short GIFs**
   1. Paste the customer's message.
   2. Pick goal, tone, and language.
   3. Copy one of three replies. Send.

4. **The multilingual proof block** — the single strongest differentiator.
   Show the same customer message generating replies in Darija (Arabic script), French, Arabic, and English side by side. No claims, just output.

5. **"Your store, in every reply"** — business-profile feature.
   One screenshot of the profile form + a before/after reply showing how delivery zones and payment methods are woven in naturally.

6. **Feature grid** — 6 tiles, benefit-first copy.
   Languages · Tones · Goals · Business profile · History · One-tap copy.

7. **Pricing teaser** — two cards, Free vs. Pro, with a single line each. Link to `/pricing` for details.

8. **Objection FAQ** — the five from section 8 below.

9. **Final CTA** — identical to hero, no new copy.
   *Start free — 15 replies/month.*

10. **Footer** — links to Privacy, Terms, Pricing, contact email.

**What is intentionally not on the homepage**
- No logos-of-customers strip (we don't have them yet — fake trust destroys it).
- No testimonials unless real.
- No "powered by" AI vendor branding.
- No video autoplay.
- No founder photo in the hero.

## 8. Five Objections — And How to Answer Them

Each objection gets: one-line reframe for the FAQ block, and a longer answer for the sales page or onboarding.

**Objection 1: "I can just save templates in WhatsApp. Why pay?"**
- *Short answer:* Templates can't adapt. DMCloser reads the specific message and writes a reply that matches it.
- *Long answer:* Templates break when the customer asks something slightly different, negotiates the price, or hesitates. You end up editing the template anyway — or worse, sending a reply that doesn't fit. DMCloser drafts three replies tuned to this specific message, this specific tone, this specific language, in 5 seconds. Templates stay flat; DMCloser adapts.

**Objection 2: "AI will sound robotic in Darija."**
- *Short answer:* That's exactly why we built this for Darija first, not as an afterthought.
- *Long answer:* Most AI writing tools were trained for English office work and only speak formal Arabic at best. DMCloser's prompts are seeded with real Moroccan Darija phrasing in Arabic script — "واخا", "كاين", "بشحال", and natural French loanwords like "livraison" and "commande". You still review every reply before sending, so if one feels off, pick another of the three or tweak it.

**Objection 3: "$12/month is a lot for my small shop."**
- *Short answer:* One extra sale per month covers it. The free tier lets you test that first.
- *Long answer:* The free plan gives you 15 generations a month — enough to see if it saves time on your real chats. When you upgrade, 500 replies/month is under 4 cents per reply. If one of those 500 replies closes a 200 MAD order you would have otherwise lost to a slow reply, the month is paid for.

**Objection 4: "My replies have my personality. A tool will flatten that."**
- *Short answer:* DMCloser gives you drafts, not final messages. You still choose the wording.
- *Long answer:* You always get three options and you copy the one closest to how you'd say it — then edit in the paste box before sending if you want. Over time, the business profile makes replies sound more like your store. It's a faster starting point, not a replacement for your voice.

**Objection 5: "What happens to my customers' messages? Is this private?"**
- *Short answer:* Messages are stored in your account so you can reuse good replies. You can delete your history with one click. We don't share your data.
- *Long answer:* We store the customer message and the replies we generated, scoped to your account only, so you can find a good reply later. You can purge your history at any time from your account page. We never share data with third parties except the providers required to run the service (Supabase for storage, Anthropic for generation, Stripe for billing) — each listed in our privacy policy.

### Top 3 Picks

**1. DMCloser** *(the incumbent — still the best)*
*Reasoning.* Names the channel (DM) and the job (close the sale) in one word. No ambiguity about what the product does, no hype, easy to pronounce across all four target languages. It's also a compliment to the seller — *you* are the closer; the tool is named after your identity. Changing it would throw that away for a small upside.

**2. DMPilot**
*Reasoning.* If DMCloser has to be changed (trademark conflict, domain, SEO), DMPilot is the cleanest swap. "Pilot" implies guidance, not autopilot — it suggests the seller is still in control, which matches the "drafts, not automation" positioning. Short, .com-adjacent, reads the same in French. Downside: loses the "closing" idiom.

**3. ReplyKit**
*Reasoning.* The most boring, least-risky option — and boring is a feature in SaaS naming. "Kit" frames the product as a toolbox, not a magic AI, which reinforces the no-hype tone. Works well if the product later expands beyond DMs (email, comments, reviews) because "Reply" is broader than "DM." Downside: doesn't hint at the commercial outcome (closing a sale), which is the whole point of why sellers would pay.

**Verdict.** Keep *DMCloser*. Hold *DMPilot* as the backup if a conflict surfaces. *ReplyKit* is the pivot name if the product broadens past DMs in year two.
